Postdoctoral Fellow
Technical University of Munich

My research lies at the interface of mathematical physics, analysis, and spectral theory. I am interested in effective structures that reduce complicated global problems to tractable local or low-dimensional ones.

At TUM, I am a member of Simone Warzel’s research group. Previously, I was a postdoctoral fellow at the University of British Columbia, working in the groups of Sven Bachmann and Richard Froese.

Research

01 / RESEARCH AREAS

My current directions include rigorous quantum many-body theory, the spectral theory of differential operators, and local-to-global principles in arithmetic statistics.

Quantum many-body theory

Bose–Einstein condensation, nonlinear adiabatic dynamics, and fractional quantum Hall systems.

Spectral theory

Schrödinger, Witten, and Fokker–Planck operators; eigenvalue estimates and the landscape function.

Arithmetic statistics

Local-to-global principles, expected values and higher moments over number fields and function fields.

Teaching & talks

03 / ACADEMIC ACTIVITIES

Teaching

At the University of British Columbia, I taught MATH 101, Integral Calculus with Applications to Physical Sciences and Engineering, in Spring 2022 and Spring 2023, and MATH 100, Differential Calculus with Applications to Physical Sciences and Engineering, in Fall 2022.

My earlier teaching activities include Analysis I and II, analysis for chemistry students, stochastics for natural science students, and a dynamical systems seminar.

Supervision

With Sven Bachmann, I jointly supervised undergraduate research on higher moments over function fields, leading to a publication in INTEGERS in 2025.

With Simone Warzel, I co-supervised Timo Feistl’s Master’s research on Mermin–Wagner theorems for systems with multipole symmetries.
